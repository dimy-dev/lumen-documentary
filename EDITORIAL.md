# Editorial Approach

## Purpose

Describe Lumen through attributable material. The aim is to help a reader understand the subject and the limits of the available record, without persuading the reader to obtain or use the product.

## Writing

Write in English. Prefer concrete descriptions over slogans, superlatives, and comparisons that lack a stated basis. Distinguish an observation, a project statement, a participant's recollection, and an independent finding.

Use dates only when supported. A repository's creation date is not evidence of the subject's founding date. A release announcement establishes that an announcement occurred; it does not demonstrate the announced behavior.

## Evidence

Performance claims need context: the original report, relevant conditions, and an explanation of what the evidence does and does not show. Do not turn a screenshot into a general promise.

Attribute interviews and recollections, record the interview date, and obtain permission before publishing a person's words or identifying details. Never manufacture quotations, users, milestones, or a development chronology.

## Scope

Keep credentials, private source code, license records, player identifiers, and unpublished operational details outside this repository. Link to third-party material with attribution rather than copying it without a basis for doing so.

Do not add sales copy, affiliate links, search-ranking campaigns, artificial engagement, or requests to buy, subscribe, or join a service. A reference link should help the reader identify or examine a source.

## Revisions

Use Git history to preserve editorial changes. Correct factual errors directly and describe the correction in the commit message. Expand the narrative when new evidence supports it, rather than adding a chronology merely to make the account look complete.
